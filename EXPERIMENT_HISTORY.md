# How these JEE experiments developed

This timeline documents **experiments**, not successive releases of a proven solver. The scores below came from different, small question sets. They should not be compared across rows as if they were one leaderboard. Original questions, answers, review traces, private references and local user interactions are not included in the Hub package.

The [progress graphs](charts/README.md) visualize checkpoint trajectories and the latest answer-versus-procedure tradeoff. The [original local 9B lab](local_lab/README.md) and [Transformers.js browser demo](web/README.md) are both available in this GitHub repository; they run different model sizes and runtimes.

| Stage | What changed | What we learned |
|---|---|---|
| Training engineering smoke | Six optimizer updates on image-plus-text repair examples | LoRA updates, loss calculation, checkpointing and restart worked. This was not an accuracy test. |
| First controlled learning run | 30 training families and a mixture of representation, planning, execution, verification and full-solution tasks; 180 updates planned/completed | Step 60 was selected **within that experiment** after 11/12 validation answers versus 9/12 unchanged. On a separately held 29-item evaluation, both got 22/29; valid procedures were 18 adapted versus 19 unchanged under provisional review. Steps 120 and 180 degraded sharply. The step-60 selection did not become a general JEE improvement claim. Its weights are preserved locally, not included in this latest-series archive. |
| Paired Recipe02 task-mixture study | 60 training families, representation-heavy and solution-heavy arms | On a separate 36-item development slice, the base got 19, selected arm A got 19 and selected arm B got 20 strict answers. Neither met the predeclared 23/36 continuation threshold. Later checkpoints developed a very-short-output failure mode. The arms' supervised token exposure also differed, limiting causal attribution. Their weights are not included here. |
| Lower-learning-rate diagnostic | Reduced an earlier solution-heavy arm from `1e-5` to `5e-6` | The short-output failure was absent at step 90 on an 18-question fresh slice. One source/key mismatch was discovered after inference; on the 17 comparable items the base got 9 and the lower-rate arm 10. This is a provisional diagnostic, not an independently validated gain. Its weights are not included here. |
| Reliable01 latest-series run | 30 balanced source-reviewed families, uniform solver-facing targets, last-three-layer LoRA, constant `5e-6` | Checkpoints at steps 30, 60 and 90 stayed generation-stable but each tied the unchanged base at 7/12 strict answers on Validation01. Step 60 reached 18/24 procedure points versus 17/24 base, below the predeclared 21/24 tie threshold. **No checkpoint was promoted.** These three adapters are included here. |
| Reliable02 coverage study | Same 30 families, LoRA broadened from 3 to 16 final language layers | Step 60 reached 8/12 strict answers and two token caps, but procedure points fell to 14/24. The arm was stopped; its adapter is included here as a failed tradeoff. |
| Reliable02 data study | Narrow three-layer LoRA retained; training families expanded from 30 to 60 | Step 60 got 7/12 answers, 16/24 procedure points and five caps. The second pass was blocked; its adapter is included here. |
| 4B baseline and verifier diagnostics | Unchanged Qwen3.5-4B/9B were compared under completion control and three verification designs; no weights were trained | Forced commitment fixed formatting on reused development data. One-claim verification fixed caps and JSON mechanics, but chemistry facts remained unreliable. The 4B training program stays locked behind canary, procedure, cell, independent-confirmation and export gates. |

The unchanged, pinned Qwen3.5-9B base is currently the default in the local JEE interaction lab. It is already hosted upstream; this archive does not redistribute it. The five included adapters let readers reproduce the **latest controlled series**, not a final model choice. The next learning question is whether reviewed atomic→composition→full-solve training views improve procedure and answer delivery while preserving the unchanged4B baseline. The [safety contract](research/TRAINING_SAFETY_GATES_V1.md) requires a new independent evaluation before any solver promotion.

The protected Eval01 and reserved 2026 questions were not used to rescue a failed latest-series checkpoint. Development sets have been reused, so apparent gains on them are not independent evidence. Model-assisted/coordinator procedure ratings and source answers still have review limits.
